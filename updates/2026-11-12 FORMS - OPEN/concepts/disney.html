Forms · Concept round · Disney Imagineering team

A form is a ritual, not a questionnaire.

Our thesis: the Daily Review is a show the owner gives himself every evening. Imagineering starts with the guest's story: you arrive tired, you pour the day out, you leave lighter, and the day is safe. So each concept stages the questions as scenes with a clear beginning, middle and seal, using the Pensieve's own world (flasks, spells, the Patronus). Motion follows the animation principles: anticipation before a form pours in, follow-through when a rating settles, staging so only one thing is lit at a time.

The magic has to earn its keep, though. Every piece of wonder here also does a job: the flask shows how complete the form is, the ride's chapter marks tell the AI which answer you were giving, and silver ink shows which words are AI's.

Three concepts below. Each one covers all five surfaces (A–E) and you can click through it. Pick a tab, then play with the mockup.

Recommended field set: just three

  1. Question: a prompt plus an optional guidance note, with a starting shape of prose ¶, bullets • or numbered 1. This covers open questions, Freeform (no guidance), the bullet and numbered lists, "Failed → Lesson" (bullets starting with "→") and Mr Nice Guy (guidance = Traits + Questions).
  2. Scale: 1–N with one anchor line per step. The anchors are guidance for you, not stored data. Only the number is stored.
  3. Checklist: fixed items you tick. Sales Progress and Life Success Tracking.

There's no separate "list" or "pair" type. A list is just the shape of the starting markdown, so a filled form is still a plain .md memory.

Concept 1

Pour & Seal

Every form is a flask. The answers fill it, and when you're done you seal it with a cork. Every Daily Review wears the same flask, so you recognise them anywhere. AI writes in silver ink, and you keep it or let it dissolve.

Key ideaThe flask is the progress bar and the identity. On the phone you answer one question at a time, in the spotlight. On the desktop, AI's words are a second ink, silver until you keep them. Edit any silver word and it becomes yours.
Why ImagineeringIt's a ritual with a seal at the end: the evening has a clear ending. The anticipation tilt, the pour-in cascade and the cork's squash-and-stretch make the act of closing the day feel worth doing.
Trade-offAnswering one question per screen on the phone is the slowest way to skim. Silver ink adds a small "keep or dissolve" decision to every AI passage, which costs a little extra effort per passage.
Concept 2

The Dark Ride

On a dark ride you board once and the ride carries you through the scenes. Here you press Record once and talk your way through all nine questions in one continuous take. Each "Next" drops a chapter mark. On the desktop, each answer is your own words from that chapter, and the AI only distills them.

Key ideaOne recording plus chapter timestamps means the phone does no AI and no juggling of separate files. The desktop knows for certain which segment answers which question. Every AI sentence links back to the moment you said it, and "Your words" is always the default.
Why ImagineeringThe guest never leaves the ride vehicle. The track of scenes, the cue card that slides in, and a "ride photo" at the exit make it feel like an experience. Talking through the day out loud, at walking pace, is the most natural way to journal.
Trade-offIt's voice-first, so it's awkward somewhere you can't talk. It needs chapter-aware transcription. Typed answers are still possible, but they're secondary.
Concept 3

Ghost Ink

Like the Marauder's Map, the page reveals itself. A form is just a memory whose questions are written in ghost ink. You write under them in your own ink, anywhere, on any device. AI never writes on your page. It leaves notes in the margin, and you pour in the ones you want.

Key ideaThere's nothing new to learn. You build a form with the slash menu, and filling one is the normal memory page with its normal Dock. AI stays outside your text, as margin notes you pour in, so your words are never overwritten.
Why ImagineeringIt's the quietest magic of the three: the questions ink themselves onto the page as it opens. It's also the most honest staging, with the AI's voice and yours physically separated on the page.
Trade-offIt's the least ceremonial, so it has less pull as a ritual. On a narrow window the margin needs space or has to collapse. Pouring notes in one at a time is extra clicks, and "Pour all" covers that.
