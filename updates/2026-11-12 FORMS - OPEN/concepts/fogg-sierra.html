FORMS · UX concept round · team 6 of 6

BJ Fogg × Kathy Sierra — the form is the smallest step toward who you're becoming

Behaviour = Motivation × Ability × Prompt, all at the same moment. At 23:30 on a flat Tuesday motivation is near zero, so ability has to be near infinite: the form needs a floor you can hit half asleep, partial must never feel like failure, and every time you show up it should feel good immediately. Then Kathy Sierra's half: nobody wants a better form, they want to be a better salesman, a king, a man with Fortitude. So the form's payoff isn't a completed document. It's seeing your own growth, with no extra thinking spent on the tool.

Floor, not finish lineOne tap counts as a logged day. Everything else is a stretch, and stretches only count up, never down.
Shine right awayThe good feeling lands within a second of the action. Fogg: emotion is what wires a habit in, not repetition.
Lowest-effort channel winsIn bed, talking costs less than typing. Any question takes voice, a photo, video or text. The desktop sorts it out later.
Reflect the person, not the toolRatings and checklists turn into a mirror: your rhythm, yesterday's lesson, which traits you showed. No streak guilt.
Recommended field-type set: 3 types plus 2 properties. Every block in the owner's Daily Review fits into these. Fewer types means less to learn and less to build.
QuestionOpen answer with a shape: paragraph · bullets · numbered, plus an optional guidance note. This covers the open question, Freeform (a question with no prompt), the list answers, "reward 🏆" (numbered), "failed → lesson" (bullets; AI keeps the → pair), and Mr Nice Guy (guidance = traits + questions).
Scale1–N with a labelled anchor on each point ("Last day of your life… 1–5"). The anchors are the guidance.
ChecklistFixed items to tick (Sales / Life Success Tracking).
◦ FloorProperty: answering this alone counts as done (concept 1).
◦ MirrorProperty: reflect this field's history back while you fill it (concept 3).

1 · The Floor — one tap, and tonight counts

Every form has a floor (for the Daily Review it's the 1–5 "last day of your life" rating). Answer the floor and the day is logged, and you get a small gold shine for it. The other 8 questions are optional stretches. They count up ("+3 stretches"), never down ("6 of 9 left").

A · Template builder

The template is written like a note: click a field to edit it in place, and use "+ Add field" (the slash menu) for one of the 3 types. The one extra control is Floor. The summary line keeps the floor honest: one or two fields, a few seconds.

B · Desktop fill by hand

⌘N opens an empty memory, which offers your forms. Pick Daily Review. Only the floor shows. Rate it and you get the shine. "Keep going?" opens the rest, all optional.

C · Phone capture-through-questions

Tap the gold Spell Menu, then Review (a new General spell). The floor question comes first, and one tap gets the shine. After that, each question offers the Dock spells: Voice · Write · Next, with Snap / Film / Previous / Finish in the conditional ring. No top bar.

  1. Tap the gold orbs (bottom-right), then Review.
  2. Tap any rating. That's the floor, and the night is logged.
  3. Then on any question: 🎙 Voice (record, then Done), ✎ Write, → Next, or the gold menu for Snap / Film / Finish.
  4. Finish whenever you like. It lands as a raw memory card (dashed, no flask) waiting for the desktop.

Fogg: at night the prompt is the phone you already pick up. The ability bar is one tap. Celebration comes right after the behaviour.

D · Desktop processing after sync

Click the sync rune (top-right). The raw Daily Review sits in the same Process list as audio. Press ▶ to transcribe, and Patronus fills the answers in grey with an accent rule: not yours yet. Keep, Discard, or just start typing in it and it's yours. What you typed on the phone stays black, and the AI's expansion sits below it in grey.

E · Memory compressing — Weekly Summary

The weekly floor is one line you write yourself. Press the Patronus orb and it reads the week's 6 Daily Reviews and fills everything else. Try typing in a field while it runs: Patronus leaves alone any field you're in or have already written ("you're on this one").

Key idea. A "Floor" flag on any field. Hitting the floor logs the day and shines. Everything else counts up as a stretch.

Why it fits our lens. It's Fogg's tiny-habit starter step built into the data model: the behaviour is small enough to do at zero motivation, and the celebration lands right after it. Partial completion becomes the designed default, not a failure state.

Trade-off. The floor can become the ceiling. Some nights the owner will only ever tap one number. The bet is that a 1-tap night beats a skipped night, and most nights "keep going" happens anyway once you've started.

2 · One Take — talk the whole review in one recording

The lowest-ability way to answer nine questions lying in bed is to talk once. On the phone you start one recording and tap → to move to the next question. Each tap drops a chapter mark. The desktop then knows exactly which words answer which question. No AI guessing on the phone and no start/stop per answer.

A · Template builder

Same in-place builder. The extras are about ability: each question gets a spoken cue (the short line the phone shows mid-take) and a time budget. The header totals the take ("≈ 5 min"). If it's too long, cut questions until it isn't.

B · Desktop fill by hand — keyboard flow

Click the frame, then press 1 to start Daily Review from the empty memory. After that it's all keys: 1–5 rate, ↑↓ + Space tick, Tab next field, and typing answers. No mouse, no extra thinking: Sierra's "reduce cognitive leakage".

C · Phone — the one take

Menu, then Review, then tap 🎙 once. Talk. Tap → to drop a chapter and get the next question. You can tap checklist items and ratings while the recording runs, and Snap attaches a photo to the current chapter. Finish is in the ring.

  1. Gold menu, then Review.
  2. 🎙 starts one recording that keeps running across every question.
  3. → drops a chapter mark (the gold rail fills up).
  4. Tap ticks/ratings mid-sentence. 📷 Snap pins a photo to this chapter.
  5. Gold menu, then Finish: one raw memory, one audio file, N chapters.

If a chapter is silent, that question was skipped, and that's fine. The desktop gets the chapter boundaries for free, so splitting the transcript is deterministic, not an AI guess.

D · Desktop processing — chapters → answers

Run it from Process. The transcript opens in the real side-split pane, one chapter per question. Each grey answer is Patronus condensing that chapter, and "Your words" shows exactly what you said. Click a field to light up its chapter. Ticks Patronus heard ("did my hour of power") show as dashed boxes.

E · Weekly Summary — with receipts

Every compressed answer cites the days it came from. Click a day chip to see the line it's based on. Trust comes from showing the evidence. You can type alongside it, and fields you touch are left alone.

Key idea. One continuous recording with tap-to-chapter. The phone captures the question boundaries, so the desktop can map words to questions without guessing.

Why it fits our lens. It maximises ability: one start, no per-question decisions, eyes-closed friendly. The time budget in the builder keeps the take short enough to do every night. The keyboard flow on desktop applies the same "zero leakage" idea for typing nights.

Trade-off. One long file is one point of failure. It needs the crash-safe chunked recorder we already ship (Snap/Record engine) and a chapter-marks sidecar in the raw memory. Talking also gets you rambling, and condensing it well depends on the AI.

3 · The Mirror — every field shows you who you're becoming

Kathy Sierra: the product is the user's growth, so show it, right inside the question. The rating shows your last nights. Each checklist item shows its own rhythm, and when it becomes automatic it graduates ("it's who you are now"). Yesterday's lesson comes back as tonight's question. The traits keep a running count of the evidence.

A · Template builder

One extra property, Mirror, whose meaning depends on the type: a scale shows its trend, a checklist shows each item's rhythm plus a graduation threshold, and a question carries its answer into tomorrow as a prompt.

B · Desktop fill — with mirrors

Pick Daily Review from the empty memory. Try Graduate on Hour of Power (26 of the last 30 nights). Answer yesterday's lesson loop. Tag the traits you showed today.

C · Phone — the mirror whispers

Same Dock flow as concept 1, but every question carries one quiet mirror line: your last 7 nights, yesterday's lesson with Yes / Partly / Not yet, each habit's count. At the end you see "showing up" (any answer counts), not a streak you can break.

  1. Gold menu, then Review.
  2. Every question has a gold-ruled mirror line: data about you, never about the form.
  3. On "Was I a king?", answer yesterday's lesson with one tap.
  4. Finish shows "13 of the last 14 nights" with tonight lit up. There's no streak to lose, so a missed night costs nothing.

D · Desktop processing — evidence, then carry-forward

Same grey "not yours yet" answers, plus two mirror outputs: trait evidence pulled from your voice (keep or drop each one), and tomorrow's question, today's lesson turned into tomorrow's loop prompt.

E · Weekly Summary — "Becoming"

The weekly form opens with the mirror already drawn: the rating curve, the habit grid and the trait evidence. All of that is plain data and needs no AI. Patronus then writes the narrative into the fields, and you can type at the same time.

Key idea. A "Mirror" property that reflects each field's history inside the form, plus habit graduation and a lesson carried forward into tomorrow.

Why it fits our lens. Motivation tops itself up because you see yourself getting better (Sierra's "badass" curve). Graduation keeps the checklist small (Fogg: once a habit is automatic, stop tracking it). The lesson loop closes the reflection → behaviour gap.

Trade-off. It's the most to build (an index of per-field history over past filled forms) and the most screen per question. Mirrors have to stay one quiet line each, or the form turns into the dashboard design.md bans.
