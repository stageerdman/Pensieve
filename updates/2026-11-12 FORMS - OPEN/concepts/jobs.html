FORMS · concept round · UX team: Steve Jobs

A form is a memory with its questions already written.

There's no form app, no builder screen, no "submit" button. You open a memory and the questions are already there. You answer by typing, tapping, or talking. When the desktop gets the take, Patronus writes the rest, and it stays obviously Patronus's until you touch it.

Three concepts, each covering all five surfaces (A build · B fill by hand · C phone capture · D after sync · E memory compressing). Everything clickable is built from the real app's stylesheets and markup: the desktop tab bar and editor column, the Process drawer, the audio bar, Patronus's Orb D, and on the phone the reel, the memory sheet, the Dock and the Spell Menu ring. The content is the owner's own Daily Review.

1 · One way inForms start from an empty memory, the same on desktop and phone. There's no Forms menu, no gallery, no separate list.
2 · Three field typesQuestion, Scale, Checklist. Freeform, lists, numbered lists, guided prompts and "fail → lesson" are all just a Question.
3 · Your words are sacredAI can add after what you wrote but never rewrites it. You can always see what the AI wrote, and a single touch makes it yours.

1

Just a Note

A template is an ordinary memory with a "Form" flag. You build it in the editor you already use, and the field types come from Markdown you already write. AI text appears in pale ink.

Team pick

Key ideaThere's no builder. Write the questions in a note and flip Use as form. A bold line becomes a Question, - [ ] becomes a Checklist, and five numbered anchors become a Scale. Every new memory then offers it while it's still empty.
Why it's JobsNothing new to learn: one editor, one way to start, and the result is the same .md memory as everything else. AI text only needs one rule: pale until touched. There are no accept or reject buttons anywhere.
Trade-offInference can guess a type wrong (the gutter chip fixes it, but you have to notice). There are no required fields or validation. A page with nine questions on it can feel long on the phone.
2

One Question

You never see the form as a page. Every surface shows one question at a time, full screen. On the phone your daily form is a single spell, and a recording follows you from question to question.

Key ideaFocus is the feature. The phone deck pages by swipe, and if you move on mid-recording, the take splits itself at the question boundary. On desktop, AI suggestions show as ghost text you keep with Tab or drop with Esc, one card at a time.
Why it's JobsThere's one thing on screen at any moment, so there are no layout decisions and nothing to overwhelm you. A daily ritual you can do half-asleep. The Dock holds only Snap, Record and Back.
Trade-offYou lose the overview and the ability to jump around. It's a new surface (a deck, not the editor), so there's more to build. Reviewing AI one card at a time is slower than reading a page.
3

Say It Once

Capture is a single take. The questions scroll past like a teleprompter while you talk, and you tap Next to mark each boundary. The desktop cuts the take at the marks and writes every answer, each one a click away from your own voice. You make forms by example from an old entry.

Key ideaTalking is the most natural input, so make it one gesture. You don't approve AI answers. You trust them through provenance: every answer carries ▶ 1:04 and a What I said disclosure. Weekly compressing shows exactly which days it's reading.
Why it's JobsOne button and one recording. A form is made from an entry you already wrote (1,980 of them are in Notion), so there's no blank builder at all. It just works.
Trade-offThe marks depend on you tapping Next, and a missed tap merges two answers. AI text looks like your text, which favours trust over control. One long audio file is less flexible than per-question clips.

Team pick: build Concept 1 (Just a Note) as the backbone: template = memory, Markdown field types, the empty-memory offer on both platforms, pale ink. Borrow two things from Concept 3: the provenance chip (▶ 0:42 on every AI sentence, plus day citations when compressing) and, once the owner has used it a few weeks, the single-take teleprompter as a second phone capture mode.

The single strongest idea: there is no form builder. A form is a memory you flag as a form, and its questions are plain Markdown, so a template is portable, searchable, syncs through the existing pipeline, and can even be made from any old Notion entry.
