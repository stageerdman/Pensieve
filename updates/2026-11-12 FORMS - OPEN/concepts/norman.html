FORMS · UX concept round · team 3 of 6

Don Norman team: forms you can predict, and AI you can trust

A form is a promise about where your words go. Our job is to make that promise visible: every field shows what it takes, every capture shows which question it belongs to, and everything the AI writes is labelled as the AI's until you say otherwise. The owner should never have to wonder "did I write that?" or "what will happen if I press this?"

1 · Your words are never editedThe AI only adds next to what you typed. It never rewrites, moves or deletes a character you wrote.
2 · The AI's words look differentEverything the AI writes is visibly marked (colour, dotted rule, source link) until you accept it. After that, a small dot stays to show where it came from.
3 · Every AI action can be undonePer answer, per pass, or all at once from the Timeline. An AI pass is a checkpoint, never a one-way door.
4 · The AI never overrides your valuesIf your voice note disagrees with the rating you tapped, the AI shows the conflict. It never changes your number on its own.
5 · Each control shows what it doesA scale looks like a scale, a checklist like boxes, a list answer shows its empty bullet. Hovering an option previews its meaning before you click.
6 · Captures land where you can see themOn the phone, every recording is visibly attached to one question, so the desktop knows where each piece belongs.

Concept 1 · Two Inks

Key idea. The memory is written in two inks. Your words use the normal text colour. The AI's words appear in accent-blue with a dotted rule and a link to their source (voice 0:42, the handwritten page). Keep turns AI ink into your ink, leaving one small dot that still says where it came from. Drop removes it.

Why it fits our lens. Who wrote what is shown right in the text, so the owner never has to remember it. The AI's work sits where it applies (in the answer it fills), and every source is one click away.

Trade-off. Unreviewed blue text stays in the file until you act on it. That's honest and doesn't nag, but a lazy week leaves a lot of blue. It also needs a markdown convention for "AI ink" that the phone renderer has to understand.

A · Template builder: what you build is what you fill

The template is edited in exactly the layout it will be filled in. Each field has one type chip. Click it to switch between the 3 types; the question text is kept when you switch. Click a title, guide or item to edit it. + Add field sits at the bottom. A banner explains how templates relate to filled forms: "editing this template won't change memories already made from it."

B · Desktop: fill by hand

A new empty memory offers your forms only while it's empty. Type a character and the offer disappears, so it never gets in the way of plain writing. Pick Daily Review, then rate (hover a number to read its anchor first), tick, and type. The dot next to each question fills in once it's answered. Inserting a form can be undone (⌘Z or the toast).

C · Phone: one question per page

Start from the gold menu → Form → Daily Review. Each question gets its own page. The rail at the top shows where you are and which pages already have something (it shows status only; it's not a control).

Dock (real 4-cell grid): Record · Next · Back (beside the menu, as the standard requires) · Spell Menu (Conditional ring: Snap, Film, Write, Finish).

  1. Tap a number or a box. Taps are the fastest answer for scales and checklists.
  2. On a question, tap the dashed box to type, or press Record, or open the ring for a photo or video.
  3. Every capture shows up as a card under this question, with × to remove and Undo.
  4. On the last page, Next becomes Finish, so the control matches where you are.

No AI on the phone. The finish screen says exactly what will happen on the desktop.

D · Desktop: after sync, AI fills in blue ink

Click the sync rune in the header, then Run in the Process list. Every step reports progress (each transcription, the handwriting read, the fill). The answers then arrive in blue ink. Your own phone text ("Rainy. Good coffee with Tomas.", "Made 40 dials") is left exactly as you wrote it, and the AI adds an expansion below it. Click a source chip to see and hear the exact excerpt. The AI doesn't override your rating: you tapped 3, and it shows that your voice said "mini high" so you can decide.

E · Memory compressing: Weekly Summary

The AI button names its scope ("Reads 6 Daily Reviews · Nov 6–12"), and you can exclude days. Press it and the AI fills field by field, showing progress as it goes. Click into any field and start typing while it runs. The AI skips the field you're in and says so ("you were writing here", with Fill it now). Fields you've already typed in get AI ink below your words. Every bullet cites its days. You can Stop mid-run and Revert this pass afterwards.

Concept 2 · One Take & the Margin

Key idea. On the phone you talk through the whole form in one recording. Each tap on Next drops a marker, so the desktop knows which stretch of audio answers which question. On the desktop the AI never writes in the answer column. Its drafts sit in a margin beside each question, next to the audio excerpt they came from. Nothing enters your memory until you press Insert.

Why it fits our lens. The layout itself is the safeguard: the AI simply has no way to touch your words. Each draft sits beside the question it answers, and the builder shows a live phone preview of the field you're editing.

Trade-off. It's the safest model and the most work. Every draft needs a deliberate Insert, and the margin needs a wide window. The one-take flow assumes you'll press Next at roughly the right moment; Re-say covers the mistakes.

A · Template builder with a live phone preview

The three types are always visible as a segmented control, so you can see every option without opening a menu. Select a field and the phone preview on the right shows that exact question as it'll appear when capturing. Edit the title or anchors and the preview updates as you type.

B · Desktop: preview before you pick

Hover a form in the empty memory and a faint preview of it appears where it would go, so you see what you'll get before you commit. Click to insert it. Move away and nothing has changed.

C · Phone: one take, with markers

Press Take once. One recording starts and keeps going while you move through the questions. The bar above the dock shows the take split into coloured segments, one per question. Each Next tap starts a new segment.

  • Tap ratings and boxes mid-take. The recording keeps running.
  • Snap a photo from the ring and it's stamped to the current question.
  • Re-say (ring) starts a new segment for this question; the newer one wins, and both are kept.
  • Pause/resume with the same Take spell. The red dot shows it's recording.

Dock: Take · Next · Back · Menu (Snap, Re-say, Write, Finish).

D · Desktop: the margin ledger

Sync → Run. Each question's margin shows its own stretch of the take (▶ plays just that segment) and a Patronus draft. Insert moves the draft into an empty answer. Add below mine appears instead when you've already written something. The bulk button says exactly what it will and won't do: "fills 5 empty answers · leaves your 3 alone".

E · Weekly Summary: evidence in the margin

Press Gather from this week. The margin fills with quotes from the daily reviews (with day chips), then a draft for each field. The answer column is entirely yours: type in it at any moment and nothing will ever conflict, because the AI only writes in the margin.

Concept 3 · Proof Pass

Key idea. The AI works like a proofreader. Each AI run is one pass: a set of tracked suggestions you step through with a review strip (‹ › · Accept A · Reject R · Accept all). Before writing anything, the pass saves a Timeline checkpoint, so Undo whole pass brings the memory back exactly as it was. On the phone, the whole form is one scrolling page; the question on the reading line is where Record and Snap go.

Why it fits our lens. The design borrows a model writers already know (track changes), so there's nothing new to learn. The strip always says where you are ("3 of 8"), and undo works at three levels (one change, one pass, any earlier version).

Trade-off. Reviewing feels like work and adds a review step to every sync. It works best with keyboard shortcuts. The reading-line phone page asks for a bit of scrolling skill compared with Concept 1's pages.

A · Template builder: slash to add, see the file, try it

Type / in the empty line at the bottom (or click it) to add a field with the same slash menu used in the editor. Two toggles make the template's inner workings visible: See the .md shows the plain file it saves to (a filled form is still an ordinary memory), and Try it fills a throwaway copy so you can check the form behaves as you expect before using it for real.

B · Desktop: keyboard-first fill with a field map

The empty memory says "Press 1 · Daily Review, 2 · Weekly Summary, or just write". Click into it and press 1. Tab moves from field to field. The field map on the right edge shows one dot per question (filled = answered); click a dot to jump to it.

C · Phone: the reading line

The whole form is one ordinary memory page. Scroll it, and whichever question crosses the gold reading line is highlighted as the target. "↳ Record and Snap go here" removes any doubt about where a capture will land.

The recording sheet repeats the question it's recording into, so you get confirmation at the moment of capture.

Dock: Record · Snap · Back · Menu (Film, Write, Next empty, Finish). Next empty scrolls to the first unanswered question.

D · Desktop: the proof pass

Sync → Run. Suggestions arrive as tracked changes. Use the strip at the bottom (or J/K, A/R once you've clicked into the memory). The conflict card is suggestion #1: you tapped 3, your voice said 4, and the AI asks instead of changing it. Undo whole pass restores the Timeline checkpoint "Before Patronus pass".

E · Weekly Summary: co-writing that waits for you

Press the orb. The AI works through the fields as tracked suggestions. If it reaches the field you're typing in, it waits ("Waiting until you leave this field…") and continues once you click out. Then review everything with the same strip.
