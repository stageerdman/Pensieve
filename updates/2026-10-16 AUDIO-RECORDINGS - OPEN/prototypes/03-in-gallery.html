Audio inside a gallery — desktop

The gallery is a justified horizontal strip: you drag a container height, that becomes the shared row height, and every image tile's width = its aspect ratio × that height. An audio recording has no aspect ratio — a 56px control does not want to be 220px tall next to photos. Three ways to resolve it, below. Drag the height slider to feel each one react. Everything plays the real 19-minute call; one bar plays at a time across the whole page.

Recommendation Option C — waveform card tile → canonical bar in a dock

Give audio a fixed tile aspect ratio so it rides the justified strip exactly like a photo — it scales with the height slider, never leaves an empty box, and the strip stays one clean horizontal scroll. Clicking a card loads the recording into a slim player dock below the strip, where the real .audio-bar renders at its natural height and plays. This is the only option that honours the aspect-ratio mechanic and keeps "one bar everywhere" intact — the player you get is the canonical bar, just relocated out of the strip where height can't distort it.

Pick B instead if recordings are rare and should always be immediately playable and prominent (it keeps the natural bar but chops the strip into sections). A is the literal reading of spec §1 ("compact bar in a gallery tile") but wastes vertical space and looks empty at tall row heights — see for yourself with the slider.

A

Compact bar tile — plays in place

The recording becomes a fixed-width surface-sunken cell at the shared row height, with the .is-compact bar centred inside. Plays inline, no extra click. This is the literal "one bar in a gallery" from spec §1.

    Pros
  • Plays immediately, right where it sits — zero extra steps.
  • Literally the canonical compact bar; nothing new to learn.
  • Transcript icon + ⋯ menu are present on the tile.
    Cons
  • Empty at tall heights — drag the slider up and watch the bar float in a sea of blank cell.
  • Audio has no aspect ratio, so the cell width is arbitrary — it doesn't obey the justification math like real tiles do.
  • A fixed-width block interrupts the rhythm of aspect-sized photos.
B

Full-width band — breaks out of the strip

Audio refuses the strip entirely. Image runs scroll horizontally as justified strips; each recording drops into its own full-width band between them, keeping the bar's natural 56px height.

    Pros
  • The bar is never distorted — exact natural height, full width, reads beautifully.
  • Plays in place; room for the transcript icon, status line, everything.
  • Immediately legible — audio can't be mistaken for a thumbnail.
    Cons
  • Chops the gallery into stacked sections — no single horizontal scroll.
  • Interleaved capture order (photo, memo, photo, memo…) makes many band breaks.
  • Heavyweight for a 5-second voice memo sitting among 30 photos.
C

Waveform card → canonical bar in a dock

Recommended

Audio rides the strip as a waveform card tile with a fixed aspect ratio, so it sizes with the row height exactly like a photo and never looks empty. A ∿ glyph, title, mini-waveform and duration read it instantly as sound. Click a card and the real bar appears in the dock below at its natural height, and plays — the strip stays pristine, the bar stays canonical.

    Pros
  • Obeys the justification math — a tile with an aspect, scales with the slider, never empty.
  • One clean horizontal scroll; audio and photos read as one family of tiles.
  • The player is the canonical bar at natural height — "one bar everywhere" preserved, just relocated out of the strip.
  • The dock is the obvious home for the transcript / split-view affordance.
    Cons
  • One click to play (not inline) — a trade for strip cleanliness.
  • Introduces a second representation (card vs bar) the user must connect.
  • The mini-waveform is decorative at small tile sizes.

Spec tension worth flagging

Rule §1 "one bar, everywhere" vs the justified aspect-ratio strip. §1 says a gallery shows the same .audio-bar with only .is-compact toggled — which is literally Option A. But a bar is a fixed-height control; the strip sizes every tile by aspect × row-height. A bar has no aspect, so it either floats in an over-tall cell (A's emptiness) or must leave the strip (B / C).

How C resolves it without breaking §1: the tile is a card (a thumbnail, like an image's thumbnail — images don't show their bar in the strip either, they show a cropped tile). The player that opens is still the one true .audio-bar. So "one bar everywhere" holds at the player level; the strip just gets a consistent thumbnail per medium. If the owner reads §1 strictly as "the bar itself must live in the tile," that points to A and we accept the empty-space cost.

Open decision for the owner: is a gallery recording a thumbnail that opens (C, my pick) or a bar that plays in place (A)? This prototype makes the height-driven trade-off visible — drag the slider on A vs C.
