Audio on the Basin — recognition & peek

Home is a field of flask cards. A card must whisper "there's a recording in here" at a glance — without a thumbnail and without a second accent. Hovering a card peeks the full memory; an audio note's peek shows the real player bar, which never auto-plays. Hover a flask to peek; flip the theme (top-right). The call peeks against the real 19-minute recording.

Recognition cue
In the peek: Space plays / pauses the recording · Esc closes · hover another flask to swap. One recording plays at a time.

Recognition cue — two variants

A · Corner glyph — a tiny ∿ where the bookmark sits. Says only "has audio." Lightest touch; survives when the meta row is hidden or the card is tiny.

B · Duration chip — ∿ 19:03 in the existing meta row, reusing the tag-chip shape. Says "has audio and roughly how long" — "the 19-min call" vs "the 30-sec memo" is real recognition.

Recommendation: Duration chip (B). It carries more recognition per pixel, reuses a pattern already on the card, and the mono digits sit calmly in the meta line. Keep the corner glyph (A) only as the fallback for density modes where the meta row is suppressed. Avoid "Both" as the default — it double-signals.

Peek playback — no autoplay

Hovering is for recognition, not consumption. Auto-playing a 19-minute call the instant a flask is peeked is aggressive, noisy, and collides the moment two cards are brushed past.

So the peeked bar opens paused with a quiet affordance: Space to play (or click ▶). The waveform + mono duration already tell the story at a glance; sound is opt-in.

Decision: peek never auto-plays; it offers a calm, keyboard-first play affordance.

Voice-memo (audio-only) on a flask

A note with no typed text still needs an excerpt to be recognisable. When a transcript exists, the flask borrows its first line — shown in a faint italic with a small ∿ marking its provenance (see "shower idea" and "that rattle").

The flask fill stays low (little text written) so audio-only memos read as light, quick vessels — honest to what they are.

Spec tension to resolve

Space is overloaded. On the Basin, Space pins the peek (Gallery.tsx); on the audio bar, design-spec §3 gives Space to play. They don't actually collide — the card owns Space until a peek is pinned, then the bar owns Space once focused — but the two-tier model needs a deliberate call so it never feels ambiguous.

Second: the real hover-peek is inert (pointer-events-none); only a pinned peek is interactive. So "play on peek" strictly means "play on pinned peek." This proto makes the hover peek interactive to demo playback; flag which tier gets the play affordance.
